Summary

Pick a material. Press start.

What it is

AnnealX is a benchtop oven built for one job: bringing a 3D-printed part to a target temperature, holding it there, and cooling it at the rate the polymer needs. The user picks a material on the touchscreen and presses start. The device runs the whole profile, from a two-hour PLA cycle to a twenty-hour PEEK run, and draws the planned and measured curves on screen as it goes.

Why it exists

Annealing is the most effective post-process for making printed parts stronger and dimensionally stable. AnnealX turns it into a recipe. Twelve material profiles ship on the device, from PLA and PETG through PA12, PA6, PC, PET-CF, PPS and PEEK to both grades of ULTEM, each with the ramps, holds and cool-down the material calls for. Sixteen custom profiles of up to sixteen steps each are composed on the touchscreen for everything else.

How it holds the curve

The controller is a model-predictive PI loop with dead-time compensation, closing at 4 Hz on a K-type thermocouple. It learns the chamber's natural cooling rate across the full range and feeds it forward, so a slow cool-down is driven as precisely as a ramp up. The heater is switched by a zero-cross triac in whole mains cycles, which keeps the switching clean. Heating is capped at 3.35 °C per minute and cooling at 1.89 °C per minute, the rates the chamber holds.

The idea behind it

The ideal annealer has one job and does it exactly: it brings a part to the right temperature, holds it there, and cools it the way the polymer needs. AnnealX is our closest approach to that idea. One screen, one start button, and a controller that holds the curve. It runs entirely on its own; WiFi is there so firmware updates arrive over the air. The engineers who designed the controller wrote the manual.
