Battery Sentinel

In development

A battery disconnect that uses the owner's phone as the key.

CategoryAutomotive aftermarket
Core disciplinePower switching & monitoring
ConnectivityBLE phone key, LoRaWAN telemetry
MarketOwners and fleet operators
Summary

Walk away and the battery is off. Walk back and it is on.

What it is

Battery Sentinel sits in the positive lead of a 12 V vehicle battery: a bistable latching relay behind a 600 A shunt. The owner's phone is the key. When an enrolled phone leaves, the relay opens and the vehicle is isolated from every drain on board. When the phone comes back, the relay closes before the owner reaches the door. Up to eight phones hold keys for one vehicle, shared and revoked from the app. A second layer watches the parked vehicle itself: a drain above the limit while parked opens the relay on its own.

Why it exists

Vehicles that sit for weeks are the ones that fail to start: seasonal cars, dealer stock, rental and utility fleets. Battery Sentinel takes the decision off the owner. The vehicle is isolated the moment it is left and whole again the moment the owner returns.

What it is built from

Automotive-grade throughout: AEC-Q100 silicon, a latching relay that draws nothing to hold its state, input protection designed to the ISO 16750-2 and ISO 7637-2 load-dump and transient levels, and a sealed glass-filled nylon enclosure. The device measures bus voltage, current and temperature, captures every crank at 500 Hz as a record of battery health, sleeps deeply while the vehicle is parked, and reports over LoRaWAN to the fleet operator's server.

The idea behind it

The ideal battery guard is one the owner forgets exists, because the vehicle always starts. Battery Sentinel is built so that every path leads back to a connected battery. Analog interlocks on the board make disconnection impossible while the engine runs. Every automatic override reconnects. A lost radio link reconnects. A button on the enclosure fires the relay coil through hardware alone, with the microcontroller out of the loop. The phone key makes it effortless; the hardware makes it certain.

On the bench

The first board revision is laid out and the firmware runs on the bench. Specifications and availability will be published here first.
