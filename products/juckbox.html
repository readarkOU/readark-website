Summary

Scan. Request a track. The venue plays it.

What it is

JuckBox gives guests a way to ask for music and gives the venue the final say. A guest scans the code on the table, a web page opens, they search and request a track. Requests carry coins: the venue sells coins at the till, guests bid them on a request, and the queue forms in bid order. A table can pool its coins and boost a track together, and a bid of five or more puts the guest's note on the big screen. Every request lands at the till first. Staff approve it with one tap and it joins the queue. A player app on the venue's Mac or PC plays the approved queue through the music app and account the venue chooses, over the existing sound system. The till sees everything live: approve, skip, refund, block an artist or a genre, clear a note.

Why it exists

People used to drop a coin in a jukebox and hear their song. That experience disappeared when music moved to a playlist behind the bar. JuckBox brings the coin back, with the bar still in charge. The guest's phone is the jukebox, the venue's own music source is the record stack, the staff at the till decide what reaches the speakers, and every coin is revenue for the venue. It runs on a QR code and one computer.

The idea behind it

The ideal jukebox is the one from memory: a coin, a song, and the room hears it. JuckBox is our closest approach to that idea with today's tools. The guest sees one screen with one job. The venue sets its music rules once, the genres and artists it never wants to hear, and the player runs all evening on the venue's own account: ambience fills the gaps between requests, and a big screen shows what is playing and what is next. Venues pay a flat monthly subscription and keep every coin. The live queue, the ledger and the moderation run behind it, so the experience stays as simple as the memory.
