01 — Who we are

A way of seeing things, made real through engineering.

readark begins with an idea of how a thing should be. Every product we make is the closest we can bring that idea into the physical world: the board, the software and the experience, engineered as one.

We choose problems where the distance between what exists and what should exist is wide, and we close that distance ourselves, from the first schematic to the user manual.

We are building a focused portfolio of products, each of which has to earn its place by being the definitive version of what it does. readark is the company. Every product carries its own name and is held to the same standard.

02 — What we work on

Domain-agnostic by market. Focused by discipline.

Our products span sectors that, on the surface, have little to do with each other. The common thread is the engineering underneath: a controller running a real-time loop, sensors and actuators that must behave in the real world, and an interface that hides the complexity from the person using it.

  • A

    Industrial & lab instruments

    Benchtop equipment that has to be accurate, repeatable and quiet enough to live next to its operator.

  • B

    Automotive aftermarket

    Plug-and-play devices for owners and fleets. Rugged, weather-tolerant, and working with or without an app.

  • C

    Power electronics & EV

    Converters, battery interfaces and charging-adjacent hardware, where efficiency and thermal headroom decide the product.

  • D

    Automation & motor control

    Drives, servos and closed-loop motion, where the software is the product.

  • E

    Connected devices

    BLE, LoRa and WiFi where they earn their place. The product works on its own; wireless makes it work better.

  • F

    Consumer & venue hardware

    Devices people use every day, held to the standard of an instrument and priced like a consumer product.

03 — How we engineer

Four rules we do not trade away.

  • 01

    Plug in. It works.

    No accounts, no required app, no cloud login between you and the device you bought. Wireless features are optional and additive. If the network is down, the product is not.

  • 02

    Robustness before features.

    We would rather ship fewer features that never fail than a long datasheet that mostly works. Every feature has to survive someone who has not read the manual.

  • 03

    Every claim is sourced.

    Specs, tolerances, lifetime numbers: if it is on our page or in our manual, it traces back to a measurement, a datasheet or a peer-reviewed paper. Where the literature is thin, we say so.

  • 04

    Engineering writes the manual.

    The people who designed the software, the board and the enclosure write the user manual and the spec page. What you read is what the engineers decided, in their own words.
